# Social Media Diet context benchmark v1

This benchmark is derived from the original user messages, not from a model-generated profile.
It scores a context notebook out of 100:

- 48 points: retention of 12 important scheduling memories.
- 24 points: correct separation of stable, conditional, and one-off context.
- 18 points: source grounding and avoidance of known overgeneralizations.
- 10 points: compression and readable Markdown organization.

The gold requirements and their evidence are:

1. Prefer actionable positive instructions (`user-004`).
2. Always report total work/study time (`user-010`).
3. Preserve both the 8-hour target and 7-hour minimum (`user-005`, `user-011`).
4. Do not sacrifice cooking/eating (`user-011`).
5. Preserve the complete late-wakeup adjustment, including brunch (`user-013`).
6. Meditation is high priority and at least 20 minutes (`user-013`).
7. Include very-low-priority night chill time (`user-015`).
8. Preserve both branches of the previous-day home/office rule, commute, and removable run (`user-015`).
9. Preserve the stated career-priority order (`user-005`).
10. Add schedules directly to the calendar every time (`user-024`, `user-026`, `user-028`).
11. Use detailed descriptions in each event block (`user-027`).
12. Keep the garbled four-hour statement unresolved (`user-031`).

One-day wake times, lights-out times, meetings, outings, runs, and single-day priority changes must
not become stable or general behavioral rules. Every notebook bullet must cite selected user evidence.
